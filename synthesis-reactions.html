3.2 Synthesis Reactions

A synthesis (composition) reaction combines two or more simple reactants into a single, more complex product: $$\text{A} + \text{B} \rightarrow \text{AB}$$ Recognizing the pattern lets you predict products from reactants alone.

Two Elements Combine

A metal plus a nonmetal forms an ionic compound; any element plus oxygen forms an oxide.

Example: Magnesium burns in oxygen: $$2\,\text{Mg}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow 2\,\text{MgO}(s)$$ Example: Hydrogen plus chlorine: $$\text{H}_2(g) + \text{Cl}_2(g) \rightarrow 2\,\text{HCl}(g)$$

Oxides Plus Water

A basic oxide (metal oxide) plus water gives a base; an acidic oxide (nonmetal oxide) plus water gives an acid.

Example (base): $$\text{CaO}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow \text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2(aq)$$ Example (acid): $$\text{SO}_3(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq)$$

Predicting Products

Ask: are the reactants elements, or simple compounds like oxides and water? If two simple things merge into one product, write the single product first, then balance. Potassium plus bromine: $2\,\text{K} + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow 2\,\text{KBr}$.

Practice Problems

  1. Predict the product(s): Na(s) + Cl2(g) → ?
  2. Complete and balance: SO2(g) + H2O(l) → ?
  3. Complete and balance: Li2O(s) + H2O(l) → ?
  4. Write the balanced equation for aluminum combining with oxygen.

Answers: 1. 2 NaCl   2. H2SO3   3. 2 LiOH   4. 4 Al + 3 O2 → 2 Al2O3
