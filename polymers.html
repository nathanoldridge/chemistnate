9. Polymers

Polymers are giant molecules built by repeating a small unit — the monomer — hundreds or thousands of times. Plastics, rubber, nylon, proteins, and DNA are all polymers; the two ways to build them are addition and condensation.

Addition Polymerization

Alkenes add across their double bonds, usually by a radical mechanism, with no atoms lost. The repeat unit is the monomer with its double bond opened.

Example: Ethylene polymerizes to polyethylene: $$n\,\text{CH}_2\text{=CH}_2 \rightarrow \text{–(CH}_2\text{–CH}_2\text{)–}_n$$ High-density polyethylene (HDPE, linear chains) is rigid; low-density polyethylene (LDPE, branched chains) is flexible.
  • Polypropylene: from propene — packaging, car parts.
  • Polystyrene: from styrene — foam cups, insulation.
  • PVC: from vinyl chloride — pipes, window frames.
  • Teflon (PTFE): from tetrafluoroethylene — non-stick coatings.

Condensation Polymerization

Two monomers join with the loss of a small molecule (usually water). Each monomer needs two functional groups.

Example (nylon 6,6): Adipic acid (a dicarboxylic acid) + hexamethylenediamine (a diamine) condense, losing water at each link to form polyamide chains — the amide link is the same bond that holds proteins together.
Example (PET): Terephthalic acid + ethylene glycol → polyester (drink bottles, fleece fabric).

Properties and Recycling

Chain length, branching, and cross-linking set a polymer's strength and melting behaviour. Thermoplastics (polyethylene, PET) soften on heating and can be recycled; thermosets (epoxy, Bakelite) are cross-linked and cannot be remelted. Recycling codes 1–7 identify the plastic type.

Practice Problems

  1. Draw the repeat unit of polypropylene, made from CH2=CH–CH3.
  2. Is the formation of nylon 6,6 an addition or condensation polymerization? What small molecule is lost?
  3. Why is LDPE flexible while HDPE is rigid?
  4. Name the monomers of PET.

Answers: 1. –(CH2–CH(CH3))–   2. Condensation — water   3. LDPE chains are branched and can't pack tightly; HDPE chains are linear and pack closely   4. Terephthalic acid and ethylene glycol
